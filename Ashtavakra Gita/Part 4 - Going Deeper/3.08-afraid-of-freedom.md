# Verse 3.8

> *Ashtavakra:* "It is astonishing that someone with no attachment to this world or the next, who can tell the lasting from the passing, and who longs for freedom — should still be afraid of freedom."

Of all the surprising things in this chapter, I think this one is probably the most gentle and touching.

Ashtavakra is describing someone who's already come a long way on the path. They're no longer attached to the things of this world, and they're not even chasing rewards in some future world. They can clearly see the difference between what's lasting and what's temporary. And freedom is the one thing they truly want.

But then something unexpected happens.

As freedom comes close, they're afraid of it.

Now, how can you want something so deeply and be afraid of it at the same time? It sounds like a contradiction. But if you really look at your own experience, you might find it's actually one of the most human things there is.

The reason is that freedom, in this teaching, means the end of that small sense of "me" that you've been carrying your whole life. It's the same stubborn sense of "me" that we talked about back in verse 3.5.

And even if that little "me" has felt cramped, even if it's suffered a great deal, it's still familiar. You know its worries. You know its habits. You know its story. So the idea of letting it dissolve into something limitless can feel less like gaining everything and more like losing the only self you've ever known.

So the seeker arrives at the very doorway they've been walking toward all this time... and they hesitate.

If you've ever noticed this in yourself—this strange reluctance right at the edge of letting go, this tendency to pull back just when peace comes close—please don't take that as proof that you're not ready, or that your longing isn't sincere.

Ashtavakra saw this in advanced seekers.

The fear isn't a flaw in your longing. It's simply the last movement of the separate self. And it appears precisely because you've come so close.

You don't have to force yourself through the door.

For now, it's enough just to notice that the one who's afraid is the very one you're being freed from.

See that gently, without fighting it, and you'll find that the fear begins to lose its footing all by itself.
