# Verse 3.6

> *Ashtavakra:* "It is astonishing that someone who has reached the highest oneness, whose whole aim is freedom, can still be ruled by lust and held back by it."

You might be wondering why Ashtavakra comes back to the subject of desire when he already talked about it a couple of verses ago. But if you look carefully, you'll see that this is actually a different kind of astonishment.

Back in verse 3.4, the person had only heard the truth. And that's not so surprising, really. Just hearing something doesn't necessarily remove all the old habits and attractions. Those old pulls can still be there.

But now we're looking at someone who's gone much further. They've reached the highest sense of oneness. They understand very deeply. More than anything else in life, they want freedom.

And yet, desire still gives the orders. And they still follow them.

That's what Ashtavakra finds so astonishing. It's not that understanding is missing. It's that understanding and craving can exist in the very same person at the same time. It's almost as if the mind has the deepest truth sitting in one room, while in another room an old desire quietly picks up the phone, and the whole body answers the call.

You can actually see this in ordinary life, can't you? Someone can understand everything about their own anger and still lose their temper. A doctor can know exactly what smoking does to the body and still smoke. So even deep, genuine understanding doesn't automatically take the driver's seat.

Now, Ashtavakra isn't saying that this person's realization is false. That's not his point. He's simply holding up the strangeness of the situation so that we stop being naïve about ourselves. If even someone who is completely devoted to freedom can be pulled off the road by old desires, then it's really no scandal when it happens to us. It's simply a reminder of how powerful these old currents are, and how honest we have to be in recognizing them.

So if you ever find yourself in a situation where you understand something completely and still act against your own understanding, let this verse stay with you. You're not uniquely weak. You've simply run into the same astonishing gap that Ashtavakra saw.

And learning to see that gap without making excuses, and without falling into despair, that's what gradually begins to close it.

And then Ashtavakra is going to show us that desire still has one more surprise waiting for us.
