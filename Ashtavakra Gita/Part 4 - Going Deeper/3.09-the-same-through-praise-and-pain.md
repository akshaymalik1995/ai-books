# Verse 3.9

> *Ashtavakra:* "Whether he is celebrated or tormented, the wise person rests in the awareness of his own supreme nature, and is neither delighted nor cast down."

With this verse, the chapter really starts to turn. Up to this point, Ashtavakra has been pointing to the gap. Now he begins to describe the person in whom that gap has completely closed.

And he starts with two opposite experiences.

One day, this person is celebrated. They're honored, applauded, surrounded by admiration.

Another day, they're tormented. They're attacked, insulted, or made to suffer.

Life brings both of these. It doesn't matter who you are. Everyone experiences praise at times and pain at times.

But through both of those experiences, something in this person doesn't move.

They remain aware of what they truly are. Because of that, they're not lifted up by praise, and they're not brought down by pain.

Now, be careful not to misunderstand this.

Ashtavakra isn't describing someone who's become cold or emotionally numb. He's not talking about someone who's like a stone, where honors and insults just bounce off because there's nothing alive inside.

That's not the point.

He's describing someone whose sense of what they are no longer depends on what's happening to them.

For most of us, though, that dependence runs very deep.

When someone praises us, we quietly grow inside.

When someone criticizes us, we quietly shrink.

Our sense of self is expanding and contracting all day long, almost like a lung that's constantly breathing in the opinions of other people and the events of our lives.

The wise person's sense of self has stopped breathing that air.

They know themselves as the awareness in which both celebration and torment appear.

It's the same image of the sea that we've come back to before.

Pleasant waves rise.

Violent waves rise.

And then they both fall.

The waves are real, and they're fully felt.

But the water itself isn't made greater by one wave or smaller by another.

So this steadiness isn't a wall that's been built against life.

It's simply what remains when there's nothing left that needs to be defended.

Now, you may only experience this once in a while.

Maybe criticism lands, and for just a moment, nothing inside you shrinks.

If that happens, treasure those moments.

They're small previews of exactly what Ashtavakra is describing here.

And in the next verse, he's going to tell us how this kind of steadiness is even possible.
