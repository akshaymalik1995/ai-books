# Verse 3.13

> *Ashtavakra:* "A steady person knows that what he sees is, by its very nature, nothing at all. How then should he look on one thing as something to grasp, and another as something to push away?"

You know, there's a quiet activity going on in us almost all the time. It's so constant that we hardly ever notice it. The mind is always sorting.

It's saying, "This is good. Move toward it. Get it. Keep it." Or, "That's bad. Stay away from it. Refuse it. Get rid of it." Every experience that comes to us gets stamped almost instantly: *grasp* or *reject*. Even as you're reading this, something in the mind is probably doing exactly that. "I like this idea." "I don't like that one." It's constantly sorting.

Now, Ashtavakra says that the steady person has stopped sorting. And then he tells us why, in words that can sound a little shocking the first time you hear them. He says it's because they know that what they see is, by its very nature, nothing at all.

That doesn't mean the world isn't experienced. That's not what he's saying. He's pointing back to what he's been saying all along, ever since the examples of the shell and the silver, and the waves on the sea. What appears doesn't have any solid, independent substance of its own. It's appearance. It's real as appearance, but empty as substance. The silver you reached for was never actually in the shell, and the poison you tried to push away was never in it either.

Grasping and rejecting look like opposites, but they're really two hands of the same mistake. They both begin with the belief that the thing in front of you truly contains happiness or truly contains threat. And when that belief falls away, both hands relax at the same time. Not because the person has become passive, but because there's nothing in the appearance for those hands to close around.

Now, you can't force yourself to stop sorting. If you try to reject your rejecting, that's just more of the same process. But you can start to notice it. Maybe today, once or twice, you simply catch that stamp coming down: *want this, not that*. Just notice it happening.

And then see something very important.

The one who sees the sorting is not the sorter.

Rest there for a moment, and then the next verse will show you what life feels like when the sorting begins to grow quiet.
