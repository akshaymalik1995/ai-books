# Verse 3.4

> *Ashtavakra:* "You have heard that you are pure awareness, more beautiful than anything in this world. After hearing that, will you still go on chasing the body's crude pleasures?"

In the last verse, Ashtavakra asked a really interesting question. He asked, if you've truly seen that you're the sea, then why are you still running around like a beggar? And now he takes that same point a little further. He starts looking at the gap itself, and he begins with one of the strongest examples of that gap, which is desire.

Now, notice exactly what he's saying here. He's saying you've heard the truth about yourself. You've heard that you're pure awareness, and you've heard that nothing you could possibly chase is more beautiful or more complete than what you already are. And yet, even after hearing that, the old pull toward pleasure is still there. The body's cravings are still there. The hunger for sensation is still tugging at you.

He's pointing to something that's actually very simple, and honestly, a little humbling. Just hearing the truth doesn't automatically change what pulls you.

A lot of us expect it to. We read a book like this one, something in it really resonates, and quietly, almost without noticing, we assume the work is finished. Then an old craving shows up again, just as strong as it always was, and suddenly we feel confused. Or maybe we feel ashamed. We start thinking, "Well, maybe my understanding wasn't real after all."

Ashtavakra is saying, don't do that. Just look at what's happening honestly, without the shame.

The words entered your ears. But the pull lives somewhere deeper. It lives in the body. It lives in years of habit. And that part hasn't heard the news yet.

So when desire comes up after a moment of genuine understanding, it doesn't mean your understanding was worthless. It simply means that hearing is only the beginning. What you've heard now has to soak in slowly, the way rain gradually soaks into hard ground.

And because of that, you don't have to fight the pull. But you don't have to obey it either. You can simply see it clearly and say, "Here's the old habit, still moving, in someone who has heard the truth."

And that clear, unashamed seeing—that's where the soaking begins.

And as we'll see in the next verse, desire isn't the only old habit that continues to linger.
