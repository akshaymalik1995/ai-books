# Verse 3.11

> *Ashtavakra:* "Seeing this world as a passing appearance, and having no stake in it, how should a steady person feel fear — even when death draws near?"

So, we saw that praise and blame can't really disturb someone who's able to watch their own body from a step back. Now Ashtavakra takes that same step back all the way to its furthest point.

And then he asks a bigger question. If praise can't touch someone like that, then what about death?

His answer really rests on two things. First, the steady person sees this world as a passing appearance. It's something that arises and shows itself, like the shell that seemed to be silver, or like the waves on the sea. You experience it, but it doesn't have any solid, lasting substance of its own. Second, because they see it that way, they don't have a stake in it. There's nothing here they're holding on to as *mine, forever*.

Now think about what fear actually needs in order to exist. Fear always needs something that could be lost. It might be a possession, a reputation, a relationship, or a future. And underneath all of those, it's this body and this life. Fear is really the mind trying to guard what it thinks it owns.

But if nothing is being held, then what is there to guard?

For someone who knows themselves as the sea, death is simply something that happens to a wave. The wave is real as an appearance. It rises, it moves, and eventually it falls. But when it falls, no water is lost. What they truly are was never the wave, so even if its ending is very near, it isn't their ending.

Now, we should be honest here. Just reading this isn't going to remove the fear of death. That fear is old, and it's deep. Pretending it's gone would just be another mask. Ashtavakra isn't asking you to pretend. He's showing you where fearlessness actually comes from. It doesn't come from courage. It doesn't come from effort. It comes from seeing what you are.

For now, it may be enough just to notice the fear when it shows up, and to ask, very gently, who is it that feels threatened?

The one who can watch even that fear... that's what these verses are pointing to.
