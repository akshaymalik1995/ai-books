# Verse 3.1

> *Ashtavakra:* "You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?"

Up to this point, we have explored the teachings through everyday experiences—anger, anxiety, comparison, loneliness, desire, and many others. Rather than beginning with the verses themselves, we first tried to recognize these teachings in our own lives.

Now we are ready to return to the *Ashtavakra Gita* itself, one verse at a time.

When this verse appears, something important has already happened. Janaka, the student, has just recognized his true nature. He speaks with great joy, saying that he is the limitless awareness in which the whole world appears.

You might expect Ashtavakra to congratulate him.

Instead, he asks a question.

*"You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?"*

It is a remarkable response. Ashtavakra doesn't challenge Janaka's realization. Instead, he gently asks him to look at his own experience.

If you truly know yourself to be complete, what is it that still reaches for more?

This question is not really about wealth. Wealth is only an example. You could replace it with almost anything: success, recognition, relationships, possessions, status, or praise. The question remains the same.

If nothing can be added to what you truly are, why does the mind still believe that the next achievement or possession will complete you?

Most of us know this gap well. We may understand something deeply while reading these pages, yet an hour later find ourselves caught in the familiar pull of wanting, fear, or comparison. That does not mean the understanding was false. It simply means that understanding and living are not always the same.

This chapter is an invitation to look honestly at that gap. Verse by verse, Ashtavakra holds up a mirror—not to criticize us, but to help us notice the places where old habits of mind continue to pull us away from what we already know.

So don't hurry past this first question.

If you are already whole, what is it that still reaches for more?

You don't have to answer it today.

Just let the question stay with you.
