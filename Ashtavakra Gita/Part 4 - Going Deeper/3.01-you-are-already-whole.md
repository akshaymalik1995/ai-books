# Verse 3.1

> *Ashtavakra:* "You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?"

We are moving to firmer ground now. You have walked the whole path, and you have met the teaching in your real struggles. So let us go a little deeper, into the actual words of the Ashtavakra Gita — one verse at a time.

Here is where the book turns. In the chapter before this one, Janaka, the student, has just seen the truth for himself. *"I am the boundless awareness,"* he says, full of joy. And now the teacher speaks again. But he does not applaud. He does something kinder and sharper. He begins to test what Janaka has seen.

His first words are these. You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?

It is a fair question, and it is not only for Janaka. It is for you.

By now you understand that you are the quiet awareness, already whole, needing nothing added to you. And yet — be honest — a part of you still lights up at the thought of more. More money. A better position. One more win. The understanding is there. But the old pull is there too.

Ashtavakra is not scolding anyone. He is pointing, gently, to a gap. There is a difference between knowing a thing and living it. You can see the truth clearly on a quiet morning, and still be tugged by wanting an hour later.

This whole chapter is about that gap — the space between what we have understood and what still moves us. Verse by verse, Ashtavakra will hold it up to the light, so we can see it in ourselves, and slowly grow more honest.

So do not rush past his question. If you are already whole, what is it in you that still reaches for more? You do not have to answer it, or fix it. Just let the question do its quiet work.
