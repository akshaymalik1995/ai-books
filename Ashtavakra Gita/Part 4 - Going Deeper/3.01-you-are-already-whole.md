# Verse 3.1

> *Ashtavakra:* "You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?"

This chapter begins with a surprising question.

Janaka, the student, has just recognized his true nature. One might expect Ashtavakra to congratulate him or explain the realization further. Instead, he asks,

*"You know now that you are one, whole, and cannot be destroyed. So how can gaining more wealth still give you any pleasure?"*

At first, the question seems to be about wealth. But it is really about something much deeper.

If you truly know yourself to be whole, what is it that still reaches for more?

Wealth is only an example. You could replace it with success, recognition, possessions, status, or praise. The question would remain exactly the same.

Ashtavakra is not criticizing Janaka. Nor is he saying that wealth is wrong. He is inviting Janaka to look carefully at his own experience. Is the realization merely something he understands, or has it transformed the way he lives?

It is a question worth asking ourselves as well. We may understand these teachings intellectually, yet still find ourselves believing that the next achievement, the next possession, or the next moment of approval will finally complete us.

Perhaps that doesn't mean the understanding is false. It simply means that understanding and living are not always the same.

So don't hurry past this first verse.

If you are already whole, what is it that still reaches for more?

You don't have to answer the question immediately.

Just let it stay with you.
