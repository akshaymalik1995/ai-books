# Lesson 11: You Are Not the Doer

By now you have learned to watch. You are the witness. You watch the body, the thoughts, the feelings. Today Ashtavakra says something that sounds strange the first time you hear it. Stay with me, and take it slowly.

He says you are not the doer. You are not the one who does things.

He puts it in a strong way. The belief "I am the doer" is like a poison working inside you, and it has made you suffer for a very long time. The cure is a single quiet thought: "I am not the doer." Take that in, he says, and be at peace.

At first this sounds wrong. A voice in you protests.

*"Of course I am the doer. I walk. I work. I speak. I did all of it."*

So let us look, the way we have looked at everything else.

Start with the simple things. Your heart is beating right now. Are you doing that? Your breath moves in and out all day and all night, and you are not doing that either. You grew from a small child into a grown person, and you never once managed it. All of it happened on its own, while you simply watched.

So a great deal of what you call "your" doing is not really done by you at all.

Now look at the things you are sure you do. You walk across the room. The legs move, the body goes, and you are aware of it happening. But look closely for the one who is doing the walking — someone separate, standing behind it, making it happen. You will not find him. There is walking, and there is you, watching it.

Then a feeling arrives and quietly claims it all.

*"I am the one doing this. It rests on me. I must push. I must control."*

That single feeling is what brings all the weight. Let it go, and the doing still happens. You simply stop carrying it.

So here is today's idea:

**You are not the doer. The body and the mind act on their own. You are the one who watches. You do not have to carry it.**

Let me be clear, so this is not misunderstood. It does not mean that nothing gets done. Things get done all day long. It means you are no longer the tense one straining behind them. The doing happens. You watch. And a great weight quietly falls away.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we will take this into a hard, real place — the moment you put something off, and blame yourself, and push yourself. We will see what "I am not the doer" looks like right there.
