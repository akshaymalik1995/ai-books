# Lesson 15: When You Compare Yourself to Others

Have you ever opened your phone for just a minute, only to come away feeling worse about yourself?

Maybe someone got a promotion. Someone bought a new house. Someone is travelling to places you've always wanted to visit. Or perhaps their life simply looks happier than yours.

A few moments ago, you were perfectly fine. Then, after seeing a few pictures on a screen, something changed.

A thought appeared.

*"Why isn't my life like that?"*

*"I'm falling behind."*

*"Maybe I'm just not enough."*

Pause for a moment and ask yourself: did you choose these thoughts, or did they simply appear?

The mind saw another person's life, compared it with yours, and immediately reached a conclusion: *"They have more. I have less."* That is simply what the mind does. It compares.

Instead of arguing with these thoughts or trying to replace them with positive ones, try something different. Just notice them.

*"Comparison is here."*

Watch the feeling of not being enough. Watch the stories your mind begins to tell. Notice how quickly a peaceful moment can turn into restlessness.

Now ask yourself another question. If you can notice the comparison, who is doing the noticing?

The comparison is changing. A few minutes ago it wasn't there. Now it is. In a little while, it will fade again.

But something has remained constant throughout. Something noticed the peaceful moment before the comparison. It notices the comparison while it is here. And it will still be here after the comparison has passed.

That quiet awareness is never made bigger when someone else fails, and it is never made smaller when someone else succeeds. It simply watches the movements of the mind.

There is something else worth seeing. The mind isn't comparing reality with reality. It compares your ordinary life—with all its worries, doubts, and unfinished moments—to someone else's carefully selected highlights. No wonder it reaches the wrong conclusion.

But even that isn't the deepest insight.

Whether the comparison is fair or unfair isn't really the point. The deeper point is that comparison itself is just another movement of the mind. Like every other thought, it comes, stays for a while, and goes.

The next time your phone leaves you feeling as though your life isn't enough, don't rush to believe the feeling, and don't fight it either. Simply notice:

*"Comparison is here."*

Then look a little deeper. Notice that while the comparison comes and goes, the one who notices it remains unchanged.

Perhaps that is who you really are.
