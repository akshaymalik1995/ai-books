# Lesson 15: When You Compare Yourself to Others

Have you ever opened your phone for just a minute, only to come away feeling worse about yourself?

Maybe someone got a promotion.

Someone bought a new house.

Someone is travelling to places you've always wanted to visit.

Or maybe their life simply looks happier than yours.

A few moments ago, you were perfectly okay.

Then, after seeing a few pictures on a screen, something changed.

A thought appeared.

*"Why isn't my life like that?"*

*"I'm falling behind."*

*"Maybe I'm just not enough."*

Notice something.

Did you choose to think those thoughts?

Or did they simply appear?

The mind saw another person's life, compared it with yours, and immediately reached a conclusion.

*"They have more."*

*"I have less."*

That is simply what the mind does. It compares.

Now, instead of arguing with those thoughts or trying to replace them with positive ones, try something different.

Just notice them.

*"Comparison is happening."*

Watch the feeling of not being enough.

Watch the stories your mind creates.

Watch how quickly a peaceful moment can turn into a restless one.

If all of this can be noticed, then there is something in you that is simply watching it.

The comparison belongs to the mind.

The watcher does not compare.

And here's something else worth noticing.

Your mind is comparing your ordinary, everyday life—with all its doubts, worries, and unfinished moments—to someone else's carefully chosen highlights.

That comparison was never going to be fair.

But even that isn't the deepest insight.

Whether the comparison is fair or unfair isn't really the point.

The real point is that comparison itself is just another movement of the mind.

It comes.

It stays for a while.

Then it goes.

You don't have to believe it.

You don't have to fight it.

Just watch it.

The next time your phone leaves you feeling as though your life isn't enough, pause before believing the feeling.

Simply say,

*"Comparison is here."*

And notice that while the thoughts keep changing, the one who notices them remains exactly the same.

Perhaps that quiet awareness never needed to compare with anyone in the first place.
