# Lesson 14: But Will I Not Stay Lazy?

We have seen a lot. The blamer is not you. The pusher is not you. And now, if
you have been honest with yourself, a fear has come up. It is a strong fear.
Let us meet it today, straight on.

The fear says this. "If the blamer is not me, and the pusher is not me, and I
let both of them go, then what is left? Nothing will make me work. I will
scroll forever. I will do nothing with my life. I will lose everything."

This fear feels very real. It feels like the blaming and the pushing are the
only things holding your life up. Take them away, and you fall. That is what
the fear is sure of.

I want to take this fear seriously. So let us not answer it with a nice idea.
Let us answer it with your own experience. Let us just look.

Inside the fear there is a belief. The belief is this: "The blaming and the
pushing are what make me work. Without them, I would do nothing." So let us
test that belief. Is it true?

Think honestly about all the times you put things off. The blamer was there,
hitting you. The pusher was there, pushing you. They worked on you for hours.
And what happened? Did the blaming get you off the phone? Did the pushing get
you to start? Most of the time, no. You blamed and pushed, and you kept
scrolling anyway.

So see what this means. The blaming and the pushing were not the thing making
you work. They were loud. They were painful. But they were not doing the job.
And remember what we saw before. The more they fought, the worse you felt, and
the more you wanted to escape. So they did not just fail. Many times, they
made it worse.

Now look at the fear again. The fear says, "If I let go of the blamer and the
pusher, I will stop working." But the blamer and the pusher were never the
thing that made you work. They were only extra pain on top. And it was pain
that did not even do the job.

So here is the idea for today:

**The fear says blame and push are the only things that make you work. But
look at your own life. They were never really working. So letting them go
takes away nothing that was helping you.**

This does not mean you will do nothing. It means the thing you were scared to
lose was not helping you anyway. You can stop doing something that only hurt
you and never worked.

That is enough for today.

But now an honest question is left. If the blamer and the pusher were not the
thing making me act, then what does make me act? Where does real doing come
from? That is what we will look at in the next lesson.
