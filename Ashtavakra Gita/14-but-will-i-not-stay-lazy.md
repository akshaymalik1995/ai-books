# Lesson 14: But Will I Not Stay Lazy?

We have seen a lot. The blamer is not you. The pusher is not you. And now, if
you have been honest with yourself, a fear has come up. It is a strong fear.
Let us meet it today, straight on.

The fear says this. "If the blamer is not me, and the pusher is not me, and I
let both of them go, then what is left? Nothing will make me work. I will
scroll forever. I will do nothing with my life. I will lose everything."

This fear feels very real. It feels like the blaming and the pushing are the
only things holding your life up. Take them away, and you fall. That is what
the fear is sure of.

I want to take this fear seriously. So let us not answer it with a nice idea.
Let us answer it with your own experience. Let us look slowly, and really
check.

Inside the fear there is a belief. The belief is this: "The blaming and the
pushing are what make me work. Without them, I would do nothing." This belief
feels obvious. But we are going to look at it closely. Because your whole fear
stands on it.

Let us start with one real time you put something off. Bring it to mind. You
were scrolling. And the blamer was there the whole time, hitting you. "You are
wasting your life. You are going to fail." And the pusher was there the whole
time too. "Come on, start now, force yourself." They were not quiet. They were
shouting. For one hour. For two hours. For three.

Now here is a simple question. If the shouting worked, when would you have
stopped? In the first minute. The blamer and the pusher were at full strength
from the start. If they had the power to move you, you would have moved at
once. But you did not. They shouted for three hours, and you scrolled for
three hours. All that noise, and it did not lift your hand from the phone even
once.

Maybe you want to say, "But sometimes I do finally start." That is true. So
let us look at that moment too, honestly. When you finally started, what
really changed? Most times, the deadline had come so close that everything
changed. Or you suddenly got interested. Or you were simply tired of
scrolling. But the pushing had been the same all day. It did not grow stronger
at the last minute. Something else changed. So it was not the pushing that
finally moved you. The pushing just happened to be standing there when
something else did the work.

Now step back and see the biggest proof of all. You do not blame and push
yourself once in a while. You do it all the time. Day after day. Year after
year. So ask yourself this. If blaming and pushing were the thing that makes a
person act, then a person who blames and pushes as much as you do would be the
busiest, most active person alive. But you are not. You still put things off.
You have shouted at yourself for years, and here you still are, scrolling.
That long history is your proof. The shouting does not make the action.

And now look at the other side. Think of a time when you did work well. When
it flowed, and you were fully in it, and the hours passed with ease. Were you
blaming yourself in that time? Were you pushing hard against yourself? Almost
never. In those good times, the blamer and the pusher were quiet. You had
forgotten them. The good, easy work came when they were silent, not when they
were loud. That is the very opposite of what the fear tells you.

So put it all together.

**The fear says blame and push are the only things that make you work. But
look honestly at your own life. They shout all day and you still do not move.
When you do move, something else moves you. And your best work comes when they
are quiet. They were never the thing that made you act.**

So letting them go takes away nothing that was helping you. This does not mean
you will do nothing. It means the thing you were so scared to lose was never
doing the job at all.

That is enough for today.

But now an honest question is left. If the blamer and the pusher were not the
thing making me act, then what does? Where does real doing come from? That is
what we will look at in the next lesson.
