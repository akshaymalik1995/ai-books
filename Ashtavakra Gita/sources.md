# Sources

## Translation used

Our reference for the text is:

**Ashtavakra Gita — Sanskrit text with English Transliteration & Translation**,
translated by **John Richards**.

The John Richards translation was released by the translator for free
distribution (public domain). The full PDF is kept in this folder:
`Ashtavakra Gita - Sanskrit text with English transliteration, Translation by John Richards.pdf`

## How we use the verses

- The lessons are written for a beginner: **one simple idea at a time, in
  plain words**. They do **not** quote the verses formally.
- The verses are our **guide**. We read them so each lesson stays true to
  Ashtavakra and does not drift. `verse-map.md` records which verse guides
  each lesson.
- Where it fits, a lesson **mentions in plain words** what Ashtavakra says
  (for example, "Ashtavakra says you are not the doer"). Where a verse would
  not fit naturally, the lesson simply teaches the idea plainly, still guided
  by the text.
- A citation like **1.6** means **Chapter 1, Verse 6**. The Gita has 20
  chapters (about 298 verses); Chapter 1 has 20 verses.

## The "Going Deeper" section

Part 4 (Going Deeper) works differently from the foundation. Here we **do**
show each verse, one at a time, so the reader begins to learn the verses
themselves. The verse is given in a **simplified English of our own** — plainer
than the original wording, but faithful to its meaning — based on the John
Richards translation. The exact Richards wording and the Sanskrit are always
there in the folder PDF for anyone who wants them.
