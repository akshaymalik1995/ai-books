# Lesson 17: When You Are Grieving

Have you lost someone, or something, that you loved — and found that the world simply went on, as though it had no right to?

Grief is not like the other feelings we have looked at. It is not a passing mood to be watched until it fades. It is deep, and it is long, and it deserves to be felt. So let us go very gently here.

Grief comes in waves. You are alright for an hour, and then something small — a song, a smell, an empty chair — and it rises up in you all over again. Your chest aches. Your eyes fill. And a quiet, broken voice says:

*"I can't do this. I miss them so much. It shouldn't be this way."*

I am not going to tell you that this grief is not you, or that you should just watch it and let it drift off. That would not be kind, and it would not be true. You grieve because you loved. The grief is that love, still here, with nowhere left to go.

So we will not try to make the grief smaller. Instead, let me gently point to something else that is also here, even now.

In the middle of the hardest moment, notice this: you are still here. Something in you is aware of the grief. Something feels the ache, and knows that it aches. This awareness is not fighting the grief. It is not trying to end it. It is simply here — holding the grief, and holding you.

It does not make the sorrow go away. That is not its work. Its work is quieter. It is the part of you that stays, even when the sorrow is at its very worst. It is what is still here when you feel you have lost everything.

You do not have to do anything with this. You do not have to feel better. You are allowed to grieve for as long as your grief needs. Only notice, when you are able, that alongside the sorrow there is also a stillness — something in you that holds all of it, and is not itself broken.

The grief will keep returning. Over a long time, it may come a little less often, and a little less fiercely. But even on the days it returns in full, that quiet awareness will still be there, underneath, holding you as it holds the grief.

Perhaps that quiet presence is what carries you, even on the days you cannot carry yourself.
