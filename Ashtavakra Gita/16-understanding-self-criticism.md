# Lesson 16: When You Are Hard on Yourself

Have you ever made a small mistake, and then spent the rest of the day being cruel to yourself about it?

You forgot something, or said the wrong thing, or fell short in some little way. And a voice inside started up, and it would not let go.

*"You're so stupid. You always mess this up. What is wrong with you?"*

Here is what makes this voice different from most. It doesn't feel like an enemy. It feels like you. It feels like you being honest with yourself, facing the hard truth, holding yourself to a high standard. And because it feels like honesty, you believe it.

So let us look at it a little more closely.

First, a simple question. If a friend had made the very same mistake, would you speak to them the way this voice speaks to you? Would you call them stupid and worthless over something so small?

Almost certainly not. You would be kind. You would say, "It's alright. It happens to everyone." So this voice is not simply telling the truth. If it were the truth, it would be just as true for your friend. It is not honesty at all. It only calls itself honesty.

Now notice one more thing. You can hear this voice. It speaks, and you hear it. And whoever hears the voice cannot be the same as the voice itself. The voice attacks. You are the one who receives the attack. The two are not one and the same.

This matters more than it seems. All your life, you may have thought this harsh voice was simply you — your own honest opinion of yourself. But it is not you. It is a voice that rises on its own, repeating its cruel and familiar lines. And you are the one who has been listening to it, believing it, all this time.

You don't have to argue with the voice, or prove it wrong point by point. That only keeps you caught up in it. Just notice it.

*"Ah. Here is the voice that attacks me."*

See it for what it is — not the truth, and not you. Only an old, harsh voice, doing what it has always done.

It will probably speak again tomorrow. That's alright. You don't have to silence it, and you don't have to obey it. You can hear it, let it be a voice, and not take it as the truth about who you are.

And the one who hears it — quietly, without joining in — has never once said a cruel word to you.

Perhaps that quiet one is closer to you than the voice has ever been.
