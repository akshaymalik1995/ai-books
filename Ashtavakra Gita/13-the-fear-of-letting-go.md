# Lesson 13: The Fear of Letting Go

In the last lesson, I said you can do your work lightly. But that was too
easy. It skipped over something real. So let us be honest today.

When you hear "let go of control," a fear comes up. The fear says, "If I let
go, I will stop trying. I will become lazy. Nothing will get done. My whole
life will fall apart." This fear is strong. And it makes you hold on tight.

I want to say first: this fear is normal. Holding on feels safe. You have
been told your whole life that you must push, must grip, must control, or
everything will break. So of course letting go feels scary. Do not feel bad
about the fear. Just look at it with me.

Now let us ask one honest question. Is it true that you control everything?

Look at your own body. Your heart beats. You do not control it. Your food
turns into blood and bone. You do not control it. You breathe all day and all
night. You do not control it. You grew from a small baby into a grown person,
and you did not manage one bit of it. So much is already happening, and you
are not holding it at all.

See what this means. Life is not being carried by your tight control. Life
was moving on its own the whole time. Your grip was not the thing keeping it
all up. Your grip was only adding fear on top.

So letting go does not mean you drop the work. The work goes on, the way your
heart goes on. What you drop is the fear and the tight holding. You still act.
You still do what is needed. But the heavy, scared holding falls away.

Here is the part the fear does not tell you. The tight holding does not even
do the work. The hands do the work. The mind does the planning. The holding
just sits on top and worries. Take away the worry, and the work is still
there. It may even go better, because now it is not shaking with fear.

So here is the idea for today:

**The fear says letting go will make you lazy. But this is a wrong belief.
Life is not held up by your control. When you let go, the work goes on. Only
the fear falls away.**

You do not have to believe me all at once. Try it small. Pick one little
thing today and hold it less tightly. Watch what really happens. See if the
work stops, or if only the worry stops.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we will go one step deeper into this. If my control is
not doing the work, then who is really the doer?
