# Lesson 10: How to See

> **Ashtavakra Gita 1.4**
> *Ashtavakra:* "If only you will remain resting in consciousness, seeing yourself as
> distinct from the body, then even now you will become happy, peaceful and
> free from bonds."

In the last lesson, we said that the old habit fades as you keep seeing. So a
fair question comes next. What is this "seeing"? And how do I do it?

Let us make it very simple.

Seeing is not a hard thing. It is not a long practice. You do not need to sit
in a special way. You do not need to make your mind empty. You do not need to
try hard at all. In fact, trying hard gets in the way.

Seeing is just this. In any moment, you gently notice, "I am the one watching
this."

That is all. Let me show you.

A thought comes. You notice, "I am watching this thought." A feeling comes.
You notice, "I am watching this feeling." You hear a sound. You notice, "I am
watching this sound." Each time, you turn a little, away from the thing, and
toward the one who is watching it. Toward yourself, the witness.

You do not have to hold it. You do not have to keep it going. You just notice
it once, softly, and let it be. A moment later the mind may pull you away
again. That is fine. When you remember, you gently notice again. "I am the
one watching this." That is the whole practice.

So here is the idea for today:

**To see is simply to notice, gently, "I am the one watching this." You do
this again and again, in small, easy moments.**

Do not make it heavy. Do not make it a big task. A soft notice, many times a
day, is enough. Slowly, this quiet noticing wears the old habit away.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we will meet one of Ashtavakra's most surprising lines. He
says you are not the doer. Not the one who does things at all. It sounds
strange, but it is one of the most freeing things he says.
