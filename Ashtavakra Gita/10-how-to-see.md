# Lesson 10: How to See

In the last lesson, we said that the old habit fades as you keep seeing. So a fair question comes next. What exactly is this "seeing"? And how do I do it?

Let us make it very simple.

Seeing is not a hard thing. It is not some long practice. You do not need to sit in a special way. You do not need to empty your mind. You do not need to try hard at all — in fact, trying hard only gets in the way.

Seeing is just this. In any moment, you gently notice: *"I am the one watching this."*

That is all. Let me show you.

A thought comes, and you notice, "I am watching this thought." A feeling comes, and you notice, "I am watching this feeling." You hear a sound, and you notice, "I am watching this sound." Each time, you turn a little — away from the thing, and toward the one who is watching it. Toward yourself. The witness.

You do not have to hold on to it. You do not have to keep it going. You just notice it once, softly, and let it be. A moment later the mind may pull you away again, and that is fine. When you remember, you gently notice once more. "I am the one watching this." That is the whole practice.

So here is today's idea:

**To see is simply to notice, gently, "I am the one watching this." You do this again and again, in small, easy moments.**

Do not make it heavy. Do not turn it into a big task. A soft noticing, many times a day, is more than enough. Slowly, this quiet noticing wears the old habit away.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we meet one of Ashtavakra's most surprising ideas. He says you are not the doer — not the one who does things at all. It sounds strange, but it is one of the most freeing things he ever says.
