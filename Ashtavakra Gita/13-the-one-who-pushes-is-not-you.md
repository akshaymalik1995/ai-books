# Lesson 13: The One Who Pushes Is Not You

In the last lesson, we saw that the voice that criticizes you is not you.
Today we look at the other voice. The one that pushes you. This one is harder,
so stay close with me.

Go back to the scene once more. You are scrolling. Along with the criticism,
there is another voice. It says, "Come on. Five more minutes, then start. You
have to do better. Push yourself. Do not be weak." This is the push to do
better. You could call it your motivation.

Notice how different it feels from the criticism. The harsh voice felt like an
enemy. It was hitting you. But this one feels like a friend. It feels like the
good part of you. The part that wants you to do well. The part that is trying
to help. So you hold on to it. You think, "At least this voice is on my side."

But let us ask it the same question we asked before. Did you choose this
voice? Did you decide to make it?

No. It came on its own, just like the criticism did. You did not plan it. It
rose by itself. And you can hear it. And the one who hears a voice is not the
voice. So the push is not you either. You are the one listening to it.

Now look closer, because here is the thing this voice hides. It looks kind.
But watch what it actually does. It fights the part that wants to rest. It
pushes against it. "Do not be weak. Force yourself." It is fighting you, just
like the criticism. It only looks nicer while it does it. The criticism and
the motivation are not enemies. They are on the same side. Both of them are at
war with the part that wants to escape.

And notice one more thing. This voice is never happy. Even on a day when you
do the work, it does not stop. It says, "Not enough. You could have done more.
Do better tomorrow." It never lets you rest. A voice that is never happy, that
comes on its own, that you cannot switch off, is not you. It is just another
voice passing through.

So here is the idea for today:

**The voice that pushes you is not you either. It feels like the good one, but
it also comes on its own, and it also keeps the fight going. You are only the
one who hears it.**

This one is hard to let go of. You held on to it tight, because you thought it
was the only thing keeping you going. We will come to that fear very soon. For
now, just see it clearly. This voice, like the criticism, is not you.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we will meet the big fear head on. If the criticism is not
me, and the push is not me, and I stop holding on to both of them, then will I
not just stay lazy forever? That question deserves its own lesson.
