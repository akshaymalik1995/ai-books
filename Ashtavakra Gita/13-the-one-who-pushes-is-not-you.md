# Lesson 13: The One Who Pushes Is Not You

In the last lesson, we saw that the blaming voice is not you. Today we look
at the other fighter. The one who pushes. This one is harder, so stay close
with me.

Go back to the scene once more. You are scrolling. Along with the blamer,
there is another voice. It says, "Come on. Five more minutes, then start. You
have to do better. Push yourself. Do not be weak." This is the pusher.

Notice how different it feels from the blamer. The blamer felt like an enemy.
It was hitting you. But the pusher feels like a friend. It feels like the good
part of you. The part that wants you to do well. The part that is trying to
help. So you hold on to it. You think, "At least the pusher is on my side."

But let us ask it the same question we asked the blamer. Did you choose this
voice? Did you decide to make it?

No. It came on its own, just like the blamer did. You did not plan it. It rose
by itself. And you can hear it. And the one who hears a voice is not the
voice. So the pusher is not you either. You are the one listening to it.

Now look closer, because here is the thing the pusher hides. It looks kind.
But watch what it actually does. It fights the part that wants to rest. It
pushes against it. "Do not be weak. Force yourself." It is fighting you, just
like the blamer. It only looks nicer while it does it. The blamer and the
pusher are not enemies. They are on the same side. Both of them are at war
with the part that wants to escape.

And notice one more thing. The pusher is never happy. Even on a day when you
do the work, it does not stop. It says, "Not enough. You could have done
more. Do better tomorrow." It never lets you rest. A voice that is never
happy, that comes on its own, that you cannot switch off, is not you. It is
just another voice passing through.

So here is the idea for today:

**The voice that pushes you is not you either. It feels like the good one,
but it also comes on its own, and it also keeps the fight going. You are only
the one who hears it.**

This one is hard to let go of. You held the pusher tight, because you thought
it was the only thing keeping you going. We will come to that fear very soon.
For now, just see it clearly. The pusher, like the blamer, is not you.

That is enough for today.

In the next lesson, we will meet the big fear head on. If the blamer is not
me, and the pusher is not me, and I stop holding on to both of them, then
will I not just stay lazy forever? That question deserves its own lesson.
