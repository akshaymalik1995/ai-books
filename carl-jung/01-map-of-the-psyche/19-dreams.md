# 19. Dreams — The Unconscious Speaking in Pictures

> A dream is a letter from the dark house, written in pictures.

Every night the lit room goes dark, and the rest of the house speaks.

Freud taught that dreams disguise their meaning. Jung disagreed: a dream hides nothing.

It says exactly what it means — but in its native language, which is image, not word.

The unconscious is older than speech. It thinks the way myths think: in pictures, persons, and places.

You are lost in a house with unknown rooms. The car's brakes stop working. A door appears where no door was before.

Each is a plain statement, once you accept that pictures are the grammar.

Most dreams are compensation at work — the night view correcting the day view, showing what the ego refuses to see.

So a dream is not a puzzle to be decoded with a dictionary. No symbol means the same thing for everyone.

The dream's water is your water; the figure in it is made of your life. Only the dreamer's own associations open it.

Jung's practical advice was modest: write them down, hold them like letters from someone who knows you, and do not pretend to be certain.
