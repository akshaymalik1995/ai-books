# 21. Wholeness, Not Perfection

> The goal is to be complete, not to be pure.

Perfection and wholeness are enemies, though they are often confused.

Perfection works by cutting away: remove every flaw, every weakness, every dark thing, until only the acceptable remains.

Wholeness works by including: nothing thrown out, everything known, everything given its place.

Jung chose wholeness without hesitation, and his reason came straight from the map.

Nothing in the psyche can actually be removed. What perfection cuts away does not die — it goes into the shadow and grows.

So the pursuit of perfection makes a person secretly worse: bright on the surface, with a swelling darkness behind.

He would rather be whole than good, Jung said — because goodness that has never met its own darkness is not goodness, only luck.

A whole person still has anger, envy, fear. The difference is that they know it, so it no longer leaks out sideways.

Such people are recognizable: less shiny than the perfect ones, and far safer to be near.

Hold the goal accordingly. Not a self with nothing wrong, but a self with nothing missing.
