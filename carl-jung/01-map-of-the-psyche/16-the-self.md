# 16. The Self — The Whole of You, Center and Circle at Once

> The Self is the whole psyche — and the center that holds it together.

One piece of the map remains, and it is the largest.

Jung wrote Self with a capital S, to keep it separate from the everyday self.

The Self is the whole house — lit room and dark floors, ego, persona, shadow, all of it as one living thing.

And it is also, strangely, a center: an organizing point that the whole arranges itself around, the way a seed organizes a tree.

Center and circle at once. Jung admitted the logic strains; the psyche is not obliged to fit our logic.

The Self is not something you build. It was there first — the ego grew out of it, the way an apple grows from a tree.

It has an aim of its own: wholeness. It presses, all your life, toward the living of everything you are.

You notice that pressure most when you resist it — the good career that feels dead, the restlessness at forty.

In dreams the Self appears as images of totality: a circle, a great tree, a jewel, a child, a wise figure.

When people said such images feel religious, Jung agreed. The Self, he said, is where the experience of God and the depths of the psyche cannot be told apart.
