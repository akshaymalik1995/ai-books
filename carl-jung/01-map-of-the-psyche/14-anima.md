# 14. Anima — The Inner Woman in a Man

> Inside every man lives an unlived feminine side. Jung called her the anima.

A boy building a masculine persona exiles more than his flaws. He exiles a whole half of being human.

Tenderness, moods, intuition, the need to be held — whatever his world calls "for girls" goes into the dark.

Jung found that this exiled half behaves like a figure, not a heap. In dreams she appears as a woman — and he named her the anima.

She is shaped first by his mother, then by every woman who has moved him, laid over the ancient pattern of Woman itself.

Mostly a man meets her by projection. He sees a stranger and is certain, instantly, that she is the one.

He knows nothing about her. The glow he sees is his own inner figure, thrown over her like a veil.

This is why the falling is so fast and the disappointment so exact: one day the veil slips, and an ordinary person stands where the goddess stood.

Her moods are his too. When a reasonable man turns sulky, resentful, strangely childish — Jung said the anima has taken the wheel.

Met consciously, she softens him and becomes a bridge to his own depths.

Unmet, she runs his love life from the dark, choosing and discarding, and he calls it fate.
