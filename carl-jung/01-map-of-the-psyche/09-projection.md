# 9. Projection — What You Hate in Others Is Often Your Own Shadow

> What you cannot see in yourself, you will see in someone else.

The shadow is, by definition, what you cannot look at directly. So the psyche shows it to you sideways.

It throws the image outward, onto another person, like a projector throwing film onto a screen.

This is why some flaws in others merely annoy you, while one particular flaw makes you burn.

The burn is the clue. Ordinary dislike is calm; projection has heat.

The arrogant colleague you cannot stop thinking about. The lazy relative you mention again and again.

They may truly be arrogant or lazy. The projection is not in the seeing but in the charge — the extra fire that will not let the matter rest.

That fire is your own exiled piece, recognized at last, but in the wrong place.

So Jung's advice runs opposite to instinct. What repels you most in others is a map of what you buried.

The test is simple to state and hard to do: when the burn comes, ask where this lives in me.

Whole nations do this too — every enemy is painted with the people's own shadow. The mechanism is the same, only larger.
