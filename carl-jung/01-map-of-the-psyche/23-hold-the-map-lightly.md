# 23. Hold the Map Lightly

> A map of the psyche is a tool for looking, not a cage for what you see.

One caution before the map is folded up.

Ego, shadow, anima, Self — these are names Jung drew on something that has no lines in it.

The psyche is not divided into rooms. The rooms are a way of speaking, and Jung knew it.

He called his concepts tools, and expected them to be improved or discarded as understanding grew.

A map misused becomes a wall between you and the thing it mapped.

The person who says "that is just my shadow" and looks no further has used the name to stop looking.

Labels can taxidermy an experience — naming it instead of feeling it, filing it instead of learning from it.

Some of Jung's lines have aged badly, and honesty requires saying so. Test everything here against your own observation; drop what fails.

The right use is the one this book began with: watch first, and let the names serve the watching.

Where the map and your experience disagree, trust the experience. Jung would have insisted on that.
