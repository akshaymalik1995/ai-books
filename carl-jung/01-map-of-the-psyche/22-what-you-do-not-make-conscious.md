# 22. What You Do Not Make Conscious Arrives as Fate

> What is not made conscious does not disappear. It comes back as your life.

This page is the map's warning, and the strongest reason to use it.

An unmet shadow does not wait politely. It chooses your enemies and picks your fights.

An unmet anima or animus runs your loves — the same wrong person, met again under a different name.

An untouched complex decides which remarks can ruin your day, and which rooms you will not enter.

From inside, none of this looks like psychology. It looks like luck, like other people, like fate.

*Why does this always happen to me* — that sentence, said honestly, usually points straight at something unconscious.

The pattern that repeats is the surest sign. Chance does not repeat; the unconscious does.

This is not blame. You did not choose what was buried, and much of it was buried before you could choose anything.

But what is seen can be worked with, and what is unseen works you. That asymmetry is the whole argument.

Every page of this book has been an act of the same kind: taking one thing out of fate's hands by looking at it.
