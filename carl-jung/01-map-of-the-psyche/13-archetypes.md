# 13. Archetypes — The Old Patterns That Shape Every Life

> An archetype is an inherited pattern waiting for your life to fill it in.

The collective unconscious is not a storehouse of pictures. It is a set of patterns. Jung called them archetypes.

An archetype is like a riverbed: it does not contain water, but when rain falls, the water knows where to flow.

The Mother is an archetype. Every child arrives ready to find a mother — the readiness is inborn, and the actual mother fills it.

The Hero is one: the pattern of leaving safety, facing the dragon, returning changed. Every culture tells it because every psyche contains it.

The Wise Old One, the Child, the Trickster, the Great Father — old patterns, endlessly refilled.

You never meet an archetype directly. You meet the images it produces — in myths, in dreams, in the people you cannot be neutral about.

When someone becomes larger than life for you — a leader worshipped, a lover who seems like fate — an archetype has been switched on and laid over a human being.

That is why such feelings carry so much voltage. The person is ordinary; the pattern behind them is a million years old.

Archetypes are not good or bad. They are the grammar of human life, and no one speaks outside their grammar.
