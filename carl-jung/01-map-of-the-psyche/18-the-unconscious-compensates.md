# 18. The Unconscious Compensates — It Balances What the Ego Overdoes

> Whatever the ego overdoes, the unconscious pushes back against.

The psyche, Jung found, works like a body keeping its temperature: it self-corrects.

When conscious life leans too far one way, the unconscious leans the other. He called this compensation.

The person who is always strong is ambushed by tears at a stranger's kindness.

The one who despises weakness gets a body that forces rest.

The strict moralist is troubled by shocking dreams — not because he is rotten, but because he is one-sided.

The tears, the illness, the dreams are not attacks. They are the other side of the scale, demanding its weight.

This is the key that unlocks most of the map's strange events. Moods, slips, obsessions — read them as corrections and they start to make sense.

Ask of any unwanted visitor: what one-sidedness is this balancing?

A person who listens gets gentle corrections. A person who will not listen gets louder ones.

The clearest channel of compensation is the dream — and that is the next page.
