# 6. The Persona Is Useful; The Danger Is Believing It

> The mask becomes a danger the day you think it is your face.

A mask that can be taken off is a tool. A mask that cannot come off is a trap.

Jung saw people who had become their persona: the doctor who is only a doctor, the strong one who cannot be weak anywhere, with anyone.

It happens through success. The mask works, people approve, so you wear it more, until nothing else of you gets any air.

The signs are quiet. You feel strangely empty after being admired.

You cannot say who you are without naming your role.

Alone, with no one to perform for, you feel like no one at all.

And everything that does not fit the mask — the doubt, the softness, the anger — has to go somewhere.

It does not disappear. It goes into the dark, and it waits. That waiting place is the next page.

The persona is a good servant and a terrible identity.

You are the wearer, not the mask — and the proof is that you can watch it.
