# 11. Complexes — The Sore Spots That Press the Buttons

> A complex is a knot of feeling that takes you over when touched.

The personal unconscious is not a loose heap. Some of its material has clumped together.

Around every deep wound, memories and feelings gather like iron filings around a magnet.

Jung called such a clump a complex. It is the original meaning of "having a complex about something."

A money complex, a mother complex, a failure complex — each is a knot formed around one old pain.

You know a complex by one sign: your reaction is bigger than the situation.

A small remark about your work, and the whole day is ruined. Something got touched.

In that moment the complex takes the wheel. Your voice changes, old words come out, and you are briefly not yourself.

Jung said it plainly: everyone knows we have complexes. Less known is that complexes can have us.

He found them with a simple experiment — say a word, time the reply. Where the reply stumbled and slowed, a knot lay underneath.

Complexes cannot be removed; they are the structure of a lived life. But a known complex takes the wheel less often.
