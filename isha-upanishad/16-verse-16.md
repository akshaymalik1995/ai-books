# 16. I Am He

## The verse

*"O Sun, lone traveller of the sky, withdraw your rays, gather in your light. Let me see your most gracious form. The Person who dwells within you — I am He."*

## Explanation

The prayer of the previous verse deepens and then explodes into realization. The seeker asks the Sun to soften its blinding brilliance and reveal its innermost, most gracious form. And when that form is glimpsed, the verse ends with the most daring sentence in the Upanishad: the Person who dwells within the Sun — the consciousness at the heart of all light — *I am He.*

## The deeper meaning

The journey of the whole Upanishad is compressed here. As long as the light dazzles, God remains an object out there — magnificent, but other. When the dazzle is withdrawn, what remains is not a thing but a Person — pure consciousness — and it turns out to be identical with the consciousness reading these very words. The worshipper and the worshipped meet, and are found to have been one all along.

This is not arrogance; it is the humblest possible discovery. Nothing is claimed for the ego; rather, the ego dissolves into what was always true. The gold hid an even greater treasure: the seer themselves.

## Central teaching

Behind the outer brilliance of the divine stands pure consciousness, and that consciousness is one's own deepest Self.

## In one sentence

The verse moves from prayer to realization: the divine Person within the Sun and the seeker's own Self are one.
