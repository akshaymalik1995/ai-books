# 18. Lead Us by the Good Path

## The verse

*"O Fire, you know all our deeds. Lead us by the good path to the goal. Take from us all crooked wandering. To you we offer our fullest words of praise."*

## Explanation

The Upanishad closes with a final prayer, addressed to Agni, the divine Fire — the light that witnesses every action. The prayer asks for three things: lead us by the good, straight path to the goal; remove from us all crooked wandering; and accept our fullest praise. It is the prayer of one who has understood the teaching and now asks for the strength to walk it cleanly.

## The deeper meaning

The confession hidden in this prayer is quietly universal. We rarely fail for lack of knowing the good path; we fail by wandering crookedly — the small dishonesties, the postponements, the detours the ego negotiates for itself. So the prayer does not ask for more knowledge. It asks for straightness.

The Fire "knows all our deeds": nothing about us is hidden from the witnessing light, and this is comfort rather than threat. Guidance is asked from the one who sees us completely and can therefore lead us truly. The Upanishad that began by asking us to renounce the world ends by praying simply for a clean walk through it — the humblest note, and the most practical.

## Central teaching

The final prayer asks the all-seeing divine light to lead us straight to the goal and to free us from our own crookedness.

## In one sentence

The verse closes the Upanishad with a prayer for the straight path — asking the light that knows all our deeds to lead us home.
