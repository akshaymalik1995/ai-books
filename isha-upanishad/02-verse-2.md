# 2. Work Without Stain

## The verse

*"Keep doing your work in this world, and wish to live a hundred years. If you live this way, work does not stick to you. There is no other way to be in the world and stay unstained."*

## Explanation

Having taught renunciation, the Upanishad immediately blocks the wrong conclusion — that we should abandon activity and withdraw from life. On the contrary: keep working, and wish for a full life of a hundred years. But work in the spirit of the first verse, without claiming ownership of the world or of the results. Lived this way, action leaves no stain — it does not bind the one who acts.

The verse adds firmly: there is no other way. Escape from work is not an option; only the right relationship to work is.

## The deeper meaning

Work itself never binds us; the sense of "I am the doer, this result is mine" binds us. Two people can do identical work — one is anxious and entangled, the other free and light. The difference lies not in the hands but in the heart's grip.

The Upanishad thus refuses the false choice between spirituality and active life. A hundred years of engaged, non-grasping work is itself the spiritual path.

## Central teaching

Freedom is found not by abandoning action but by acting without ownership, which alone keeps the actor unstained.

## In one sentence

The verse teaches that a full life of work binds no one, so long as the work is done without claiming its fruits.
