# 6. The End of Hatred

## The verse

*"One who sees all beings in the Self, and the Self in all beings, never turns away from anything."*

## Explanation

This verse gives the practical fruit of the vision the Upanishad has been building. When a person sees that all beings exist within the one Self, and that the same Self dwells in every being, something remarkable happens: aversion ends. There is no longer anyone to despise, no one to shrink from, nothing to turn away from — because everywhere one looks, one meets oneself.

## The deeper meaning

Hatred and disgust require distance: there must be an "other" over there, fundamentally different from me. The vision of oneness removes the distance at its root. The difficult person, the stranger, the enemy — each becomes the same consciousness, wearing a different face and history.

This does not mean approving of every action or losing all discernment. It means the basic recoil from other beings dies away. One can still oppose a deed while no longer hating the doer, in the way we oppose our own mistakes without hating ourselves.

## Central teaching

Seeing the one Self in all beings dissolves aversion, because there is no longer any true "other" to turn away from.

## In one sentence

The verse teaches that one who sees the same Self everywhere can no longer hate or recoil from anyone.
