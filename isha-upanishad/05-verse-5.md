# 5. Far and Near

## The verse

*"It moves, and it does not move. It is far, and it is near. It is within all this, and it is outside all this."*

## Explanation

The paradoxes continue, now in their most compact form. The Self moves and does not move; it is far and near; inside everything and outside everything. Ordinary language is built for objects, which are either here or there, still or moving. The Self is not an object, so every one-sided statement about it is false, and the Upanishad corrects each statement with its opposite.

## The deeper meaning

The Self is far for the one who seeks it as a distant goal — lifetimes away, unreachable. It is near for the one who notices it as their own awareness — nearer than breath. It moves as all the life of the world; it stands still as the silent witness of that life. Both descriptions are true, depending on where we stand.

This is also why sincere seekers can feel the truth is impossibly remote one day and intimately close the next. The distance was never in the Self; it was in the direction of the looking.

## Central teaching

The Self escapes every category — far and near, moving and still — because it is the one reality that both contains and inhabits all things.

## In one sentence

The verse teaches that the Self is the farthest thing when sought outside and the nearest thing when recognized within.
